He initialization and vanishing gradients

These are short lecture notes for Prince (2023) section 7.5.

For two layers in a MLP with RELU nonlinearity \(\phi\), the map between adjacent preactivations \(f^{t}\) and \(f^{t + 1}\), with dimensions \(N\) and \(M\) respectively, takes the form \[ f^{t + 1}= \boldsymbol{\beta}+ \boldsymbol{A}\phi(f^{t}). \] Let’s suppose for the sake of an example that \(f^{t}\) is multivariate normal, with mean \(\boldsymbol{0}\) and covariance \(s^2 I_{N \times N}\), that \(\boldsymbol{\beta}= \boldsymbol{0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{A}\) are all IID normals with mean zero and variance \(\sigma^2\). We can then ask what the variance of \(f^{t + 1}\) is. First, note that because \(\boldsymbol{A}\) and \(f^{t}\) are independent, \[ \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[f^{t + 1}\right] = \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[\boldsymbol{A}\right] \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[\phi(f^{t})\right] = \boldsymbol{0}. \] So the variance of the \(m\)–th entry of \(f^{t + 1}\) is \(\mathrm{Var}_{\,}\left({f^{t + 1}}_m\right) = \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[{f^{t + 1}_m}^2\right]\). Again, since \(\boldsymbol{A}\), and \(f^{t}\) are independent, \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[{f^{t + 1}_m}^2\right] ={}& \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[\left(\sum_{n=1}^N \boldsymbol{A}_{mn} \phi_m(f^{t}) \right)^2\right] ={}& \sum_{n=1}^N \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[\boldsymbol{A}_{mn}^2\right] \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[\phi_m(f^{t})^2\right] \\={}& \sigma^2 \sum_{n=1}^N \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[\phi_m(f^{t})^2\right]. \end{aligned} \] Since each element of \(f^{t}\) is equally likely to be positive or negative, \(\mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[\phi_m(f^{t})^2\right] = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[{f^{t}}^2\right] = \frac{1}{2} s^2\), giving the final expression \[ \mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[f^{t + 1}_m\right] = \frac{N \sigma^2}{2} s^2. \] That is, the variance of each subsequent hidden layer increases by a factor of \(N \sigma^2\).

Similarly, if we are doing reverse mode backprobagation and have computed \({\boldsymbol{v}^{t + 1}}^\intercal= \frac{d y}{d {f^{t + 1}}^\intercal}\), then \[ {\boldsymbol{v}^{t}}^\intercal:= \frac{d y}{d {f^{t}}^\intercal} = \frac{d y}{d {f^{t + 1}}^\intercal} \frac{d f^{t + 1}}{d {f^{t}}^\intercal} = {\boldsymbol{v}^{t + 1}}^\intercal\boldsymbol{A}\frac{d \phi(f^{t})}{d {f^{t}}^\intercal}. \] The diagonal entries of \(\frac{d \phi(f^{t})}{d {f^{t}}^\intercal}\) are either one or zero, each with equal probability, according to whether the RELU is active. Assuming as above that \(\mathbb{E}_{\vphantom{}}\left[\boldsymbol{v}^{t + 1}\right] = \boldsymbol{0}\) and \(\mathrm{Cov}_{\,}\left(\boldsymbol{v}^{t + 1}\right) = I_{M \times M} s^2\), we see that the variance of the \(n\)–th entry of \(\boldsymbol{v}^{t}\) is \(\frac{M \sigma^2}{2} s^2\). This backward pass version of the same formula is known as “exploding gradients”.

This recommends selecting the intial parameters to be inversely proportional to the dimension of the hidden layer. Taking \(\sigma^2 \propto N / 2\) is known as “He initialziation,” or “Kaiming” initialization to avoid pronunciation problems for English speakers.

Adam and momentum

These are short lecture notes for Prince (2023) section 6.4.

Recall gradient (or stochastic gradient descent) on \(f(\theta)\), with \(g(\theta) := \frac{\partial f(\theta)}{\partial \theta}\): \[ \theta^{t + 1}= \theta^{t}- \alpha g^{t}(\theta^{t}). \]

Momentum usues some information from the previous step, taking \[ \tilde{g}^{t}= \beta \tilde{g}^{t - 1}+ (1-\beta) g^{t}(\theta^{t}) \textrm{ and } \theta^{t + 1}= \theta^{t}- \alpha \tilde{g}^{t}. \] Note that rescaling change the effective step size, without actually changing the geometry of the problem. One way to avoid this is to take a step normalized by the squared size of the gradient step. You could also estimate this squared size with momentum; this, together with a modification of the initialization gives the Adam (Adaptive momentum estimation) algorithm.
